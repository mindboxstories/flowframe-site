Best Webcams for Video Calls in 2026: 7 Picks From $25 to $199

FlowFrame teamUpdated 13 Nov 2026Prices checked Nov 2026

Quick answer: For most people working from home, the Anker PowerConf C200 (about $60) is the best value: sharp 2K video, a privacy shutter and decent mics. If you want the safest, most familiar choice, get the Logitech C920s Pro. For a noticeably better picture on every call, the Logitech Brio 500 and the Insta360 Link 2C are the step up. On a tight budget, a $25 Logitech C310 still beats most built-in laptop cameras.

1. Anker PowerConf C200: best value for most people

The C200 is the budget pick at both Tom's Guide and Engadget, and it is easy to see why. You get 2K video, fast autofocus, a field of view you can narrow from 95 to 78 or 65 degrees (handy if your room is messy), two stereo microphones and a sliding privacy cover, for around $60.

Choose it if you want a clearly sharper picture than your laptop camera without paying for 4K.

Skip it if you need to angle the camera a lot: it doesn't swivel and the cable is short.

2. Logitech C920s Pro: the safe, classic choice

The C920 family has been the default office webcam for years, and TechRadar still rates the C920s Pro its best overall budget webcam. It shoots 1080p at 30fps with reliable autofocus, has stereo mics and adds a privacy shutter. It works with Zoom, Teams and Google Meet the moment you plug it in.

Choose it if you want something proven that IT departments and colleagues already use.

Skip it if you want to zoom out or in: the field of view is fixed, and the design is showing its age.

3. Logitech Brio 500: best for everyday calls

Engadget's best webcam overall. The Brio 500 stays at 1080p, but it handles dim rooms well thanks to automatic light correction, lets you pick a 90, 78 or 65-degree view, and can keep you centred with auto-framing. It has a built-in lens cover and connects by USB-C.

Choose it if you are on camera for hours a day, often in a room with poor light.

Skip it if you are on a budget; the C200 gets you most of the way for less than half the price.

4. Insta360 Link 2C: best 4K for the money

Tom's Guide's best webcam of 2026. The Link 2C gives a very clear 4K image with good colour and solid low-light performance, plus detailed companion software, at around $149. Its own microphone sounds compressed, though, so pair it with a headset or a USB mic.

Choose it if picture quality matters most, for client calls, interviews or recording training videos.

Skip it if you rely on the webcam's microphone; you will want a separate mic.

5. Obsbot Meet Flip: best for laptops and travel

A tiny 4K camera that weighs about 37 g (1.3 oz) and flips its lens down for privacy. It clips onto a laptop lid and is a big step up from a built-in laptop camera. Tom's Guide lists it at around $119, often $99 on sale.

Choose it if you work from a laptop and move between home, the office and hotels.

Skip it if you need great audio: the mic is only decent, and auto-tracking loses you if you step out of frame.

6. Logitech MX Brio 705: the premium Logitech

Logitech's top business webcam shoots 4K at 30fps, has beamforming mics, AI-assisted autofocus and white balance, and a choice of 65, 78 or 90-degree views, all set up through the Logi Tune app. Expect around $199.

Choose it if you want the best Logitech picture and already use Logitech keyboards and mice.

Skip it if you work from a laptop on battery: it gets warm in long sessions, drains power and is heavy at 174 g.

7. Logitech C310: the cheapest real upgrade

If your laptop camera is grainy and you just need something better today, the C310 does 720p at 30fps with automatic light correction and a noise-reducing mic for around $25, sometimes $21. It is basic, but for occasional calls it does the job.

Choose it if you need a cheap camera for a desktop PC that has none, or a spare for a second desk.

Skip it if you are on video every day; spend about $35 more on the C200 or C920s.

How to choose in 30 seconds

Want the best value? Anker PowerConf C200.

Want the safe, familiar option? Logitech C920s Pro.

On calls all day in a dim room? Logitech Brio 500.

Want a 4K picture? Insta360 Link 2C, with a separate mic.

Working from a laptop on the move? Obsbot Meet Flip.

Spending as little as possible? Logitech C310.

Three free fixes before you buy

Face a window, not away from it. Light in front of you does more for your picture than any upgrade in this list. Light behind you turns you into a silhouette.

Raise the camera to eye level. A stack of books under your laptop stops people looking up your nose.

Use headphones. Even a cheap wired pair stops echo, which bothers people on the call far more than a soft picture.
